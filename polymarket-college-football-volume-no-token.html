Prediction Markets Are Having a Football-Season Explosion. There's Still No Polymarket Token.

Billions of dollars are moving through blockchain-settled betting markets every week this football season. The platform gaining the most ground still hasn't launched the token everyone's positioning for.

Published Sept 21, 2026 · 6 min read · Culture Desk

College football prediction market trading has reached $790 million this season. Kalshi holds the largest share at roughly 69.9%, with Polymarket in second place, Novig at 4.3%, DraftKings Predictions at 3.5%, and Underdog around 1%. Those numbers alone would make football-season prediction markets a notable story. What makes it a PumpReport story is how fast the balance of power inside that market is shifting, and what's still missing from one side of it.

The growth numbers are startling even by crypto standards

In the single week of September 7 to 13, 2026, Polymarket's global site saw its NFL contract volume rise 4,399% week-over-week to $27.62 million. Its newly CFTC-regulated U.S. arm — operating under the QCX exchange license Polymarket acquired in mid-2025 — grew 1,760% to $23.20 million over the same week. Total sports volume across Polymarket's global platform rose 104.7% week-over-week to roughly $499 million. Combined with Kalshi, the two platforms did $14.1 billion in prediction-market volume in that single September week; Kalshi alone generated approximately $983 million in NFL volume during Week 1 of the season. Within months of relaunching in the U.S., Polymarket has already captured an estimated 43.8% of Kalshi's football trading volume — a fast erosion of a dominant incumbent's lead.

This runs on blockchain rails, whether or not it looks like it

Neither Polymarket nor Kalshi markets itself primarily as a crypto product to the average sports bettor scrolling odds on a college football Saturday. But Polymarket's markets settle on Polygon, denominated and paid out in USDC — genuine on-chain infrastructure processing genuine blockchain-native volume, at a scale (hundreds of millions of dollars a week) that dwarfs most individual token ecosystems covered on this site. That's exactly the kind of underlying adoption crypto proponents have argued blockchain rails would eventually capture, arriving through a sports-betting front door rather than a "buy crypto" one.

The token that keeps not arriving

Here's the part that makes this a PumpReport story instead of a sports-betting story: Polymarket still doesn't have its own token. CEO Shayne Coplan previously posted a "POLY" ticker alongside Bitcoin and Ethereum on X, widely read as a signal an announcement was coming. Reporting at the time also indicated the company planned to issue token warrants to investors as part of a funding round — giving those investors the option to purchase a token once one exists. None of that has yet produced an actual launch, even as the platform's on-chain volume has scaled into the billions per week and its valuation has climbed (Intercontinental Exchange reportedly invested $2 billion at a $9 billion valuation).

That combination — massive, growing, blockchain-settled volume and a widely anticipated but still-unlaunched token — is structurally similar to the pre-launch speculation dynamic covered in our reporting on LAPTOP's fake tokens rallying before the real launch: anticipation itself becomes a trading narrative well before there's an actual token to buy. The difference here is scale. If and when a Polymarket token does launch, it would be arriving on top of a platform already doing tens of billions in annualized volume, rather than the reverse.
