Inside the $648 Million Short Squeeze That Pushed Bitcoin to $85K

Bitcoin ripped higher today on forced buying, not fresh conviction — and the data shows leveraged traders piled right back in behind it.

Published Sept 21, 2026 · 5 min read · Markets Desk
Data as of Sept 21, 2026, ~time of publication. Prices, open interest, and liquidation totals change continuously — check a live source before acting.

Bitcoin traded at $84,984 today, up 5.4% over 24 hours, after a wave of forced short covering tore through the derivatives market. Per CoinDesk's reporting on Coinglass data, $746.6 million in total positions were liquidated over 24 hours, with $647.9 million of that coming from short positions — traders who had bet the price would fall and got run over when it didn't. In the single hour before the report, another $159.9 million was liquidated, roughly 95% of it shorts. Bitcoin-specific shorts accounted for $277.5 million of the day's total; Ether shorts added another $122.8 million.

The move pushed BTC above its September 4 high of $82,284, making today's price the top of the month's range so far.

The mechanism: how a short squeeze feeds itself

A short position is a bet that price falls — a trader borrows and sells an asset, planning to buy it back cheaper later. When price rises instead, exchanges force those traders to buy back at a loss once their collateral runs out, and that forced buying pushes price up further, which can trigger the next round of forced buying below it. It's a mechanical cascade, not new demand showing up because someone decided Bitcoin got more valuable — which is exactly why the data underneath today's move is worth a closer look than the headline price.

The tell: open interest went up, not down

If today's rally were simply the market de-risking — shorts closing and leverage draining out of the system — total open interest (the sum of all outstanding leveraged positions) should have fallen. Instead, per CoinDesk, open interest climbed 7.6% to $156 billion during the squeeze, while 24-hour trading volume surged 39% to $224 billion. That combination means traders weren't stepping back from leverage after getting liquidated — they were replacing the closed short positions with new ones, mostly long, about as fast as the shorts were getting wiped out.

Binance's whale account data adds a specific texture to that: the exchange's whale long/short ratio sat around 1.0 — roughly balanced — while the exchange's overall derivatives positioning exceeded 2.0. Read together, that suggests large accounts are structurally holding heavy leveraged long exposure even as smaller retail-style positioning stays closer to even. The CD100 index, a broad market gauge, rose 3.0% with 95 of its 100 constituents gaining, and options markets showed increased call buying alongside what CoinDesk described as "orderly" implied volatility — not panic, but a market leaning hard in one direction.

What this means if you're not the one getting squeezed

A price move built on forced short covering, with open interest rising rather than falling behind it, is a market that's gotten more leveraged during the rally, not less. That's not automatically bearish — leverage can keep building in a genuine uptrend for a long time — but it does mean the same mechanism that just squeezed shorts higher can run in reverse if sentiment turns, since a market this stacked with longs is itself vulnerable to a long squeeze on the way back down. This is the same dynamic covered in our look at what the order book looks like before a breakout — thin, one-sided positioning cuts both ways.
