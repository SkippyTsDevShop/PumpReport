How to Spot the Next PEPE (Before Everyone Else Does)

PEPE turned a frog meme into a multi-billion-dollar asset. Thousands of copycats have tried to repeat it and gone to zero. Here's the actual framework for telling the difference early.

Published Sept 14, 2026 · 7 min read · Culture Desk

Pepe the Frog (PEPE) is currently sitting around a $1.45 billion market capitalization, according to data cited by CoinGape in September 2026 — trailing only Dogecoin (~$13.09B) and Shiba Inu (~$3.08B) among meme coins by that measure. It got there with no presale, no venture round, and no team token allocation most holders could point to. That combination is rare, and it's a big part of why PEPE still gets treated as the reference case rather than just another meme coin that pumped once.

Every cycle since, new tokens have tried to manufacture the same outcome on purpose. Almost none of them do. Here's what actually separates a meme coin with a real shot from one that's just recycling the aesthetic.

1. Distribution, not marketing

The single biggest structural difference between PEPE-style launches and most of what launches today is who holds the supply on day one. A fair-launch token with no presale and no team allocation means early buyers are competing with each other, not with insiders who bought at a fraction of the public price. Check the chain: block explorers and tools like Bubblemaps or the token page on Etherscan/Solscan will show you top-holder concentration. A handful of wallets holding a large share of supply is a structural headwind, no matter how good the marketing looks.

2. Where the liquidity actually sits

Locked liquidity — meaning the trading pair's liquidity pool tokens are locked or burned, not held by the deployer — matters more than almost any other single factor. An unlocked liquidity pool means the deployer can pull it at any time, a pattern commonly called a "rug." Tools like Token Sniffer and DEX Screener's security tab surface this in seconds. If you can't verify it, that itself is the signal.

3. Organic community vs. paid community

PEPE's early growth came from meme culture that existed independent of the token — the frog was already a widely used internet symbol before it had a ticker. Coins trying to manufacture that from scratch usually lean on paid influencer threads and bot-inflated engagement instead. A rough gut check: look at whether the same fifteen accounts are the entire conversation, or whether unaffiliated crypto communities are talking about the token unprompted. The second pattern is much rarer and much more valuable.

4. Chain and timing

Where a token launches shapes who can realistically trade it. Solana-based meme coins like Bonk (BONK) and Dogwifhat (WIF) built momentum in part because Solana's low fees let smaller traders enter and exit cheaply, which matters enormously for meme coin liquidity depth. A token launching into a chain with high gas costs and thin DEX liquidity is fighting an uphill battle regardless of the meme.

5. Utility is a plus, not a requirement — but it changes the shape of the trade

Newer entrants like MemeToro, which combines a Pepe-adjacent mascot with AI-agent functionality on BNB Chain, and Pudgy Penguins (PENGU), which bridges an existing NFT brand into gaming and merchandise, represent a different bet than pure-meme plays: the idea that a coin with an actual product roadmap holds attention longer between hype cycles. That doesn't make them safer — utility claims can be just as overstated as meme claims — but it does mean the relevant diligence question shifts from "is the meme funny" to "does the roadmap have anything shipped."

The honest version of this

None of this is a formula that reliably calls the next 100x. Nobody has one, and anyone selling you one is the pattern, not the exception. What this framework does is filter out the launches with obvious structural red flags — concentrated supply, unlocked liquidity, manufactured community — before you ever get to the harder question of whether the meme itself has real staying power. That second question stays genuinely unpredictable, for PEPE's copycats and for whatever launches next.
